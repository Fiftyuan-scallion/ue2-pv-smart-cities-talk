02 · Research questions

Three parts, three separate literatures — geography missing from the centre

RQ1. How do geographic factors — solar radiation, cloud cover and climate — shape solar resource assessment and the energy management of photovoltaic systems in smart cities?
RQ2. How can satellite-based radiation data and short-term photovoltaic forecasting, informed by local geographic conditions, improve urban energy management and grid stability?

03 · Theme A

Geographic assessment — how much energy, and where to build

  • Zhang et al. (2022) — satellite cloud climatology (GLHCC, 1 km) estimates PV energy across Chinahigh resolution beats old products where clouds change fast
  • Doljak & Stanojević (2017) — GIS + multi-criteria (AHP) site selection, Serbianorth / Banat region holds the largest solar potential
  • One measures "how much" — the other decides "where"

06 · Evaluation & implications

One method, deepened — and what it still misses

  • Deep dive: Zhang's satellite cloud method reveals real urban unevenness of solar resources
  • But: it stops at assessment — never feeds forecasting or management
  • Implications: planners choose sites by data · geography belongs at the centre · one linked framework is the next step
For a geography student: GIS & remote sensing are the bridge between environmental data and urban energy planning.
