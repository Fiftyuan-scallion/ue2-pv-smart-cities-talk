02 · Research questions

Three parts, three separate literatures — geography missing from the centre

RQ1. How do geographic factors — solar radiation, cloud cover and climate — shape solar resource assessment and the energy management of photovoltaic systems in smart cities?
RQ2. How can satellite-based radiation data and short-term photovoltaic forecasting, informed by local geographic conditions, improve urban energy management and grid stability?

03 · Theme A

Assessment — how much energy, and where to build

  • Zhang et al. (2022) — satellite cloud climatology on a 1 km grid estimates PV energy across Chinafine resolution beats coarse products where cloud varies fast
  • Doljak & Stanojević (2017) — GIS plus multi-criteria (AHP) site ranking for Serbiathe northern Banat region carries the largest potential
  • One answers "how much" — the other answers "where"
Cloud is the filter. Satellite climatology turns cloud patterns into a usable solar resource map — the method behind Theme A.

04 · Theme B

Forecasting — local climate beats a global model

  • Sanjari & Gooi (2017) — local temperature and irradiance define the operating statehigher-order Markov chain gives a 15-minute probabilistic forecast
  • Singla et al. (2022) — review across physical, statistical and machine-learning methodsartificial neural networks usually come out on top
  • One proves "local matters" — the other maps "how it is done"

06 · Evaluation & implications

One method, deepened — and what it still misses

  • Deep dive: Zhang's satellite method exposes real unevenness in urban solar resources
  • But: it stops at assessment — never feeding forecasting or dispatch
  • Implications: site on evidence · keep geography central · link the three stages
As a geography student: GIS and remote sensing are the bridge between environmental data and urban energy planning.
